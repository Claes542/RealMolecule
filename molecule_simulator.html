Molecule Simulator

RealQM, hard partition: every electron (or opposite-spin pair, 2hom) owns a domain; domains meet at free walls placed by density continuity. Heavy atoms are pseudo-atoms: inner shells frozen into a core of charge Zx with hard radius rc fitted to the sum of the ionisation energies of the electrons carried. Solver molecule.js (WebGPU), box 12 a0 (LiH 20), h = 0.1.
Judged by geometry. Bond lengths and shapes are the result; binding (atomisation) energies are shown as a diagnostic. Where an atom carries many valence electrons the binding carries a large offset set by the composition and nearly independent of geometry (CH4: 3.2 Eh, constant to 0.03 over R 2.05–2.45), which cancels at fixed composition and in processes that keep every atom's bonding.

Energy offset and the range

A free wall between domains carries no Pauli kinetic energy (the cost, in standard quantum mechanics, of the nodes that keep electrons of the same spin apart). Where a heavy atom carries many electrons around it this missing term shows as an energy offset: CH4 with pseudo-C +4 over-binds by 3.2 Eh, NH3 by 1.4, and on forming CH4 the kinetic energy falls where the virial theorem requires it to rise. In CH4 the offset hardly depends on geometry, so the bond (+10%) and the shape (tetrahedral below square planar, rows above) survive. Where lone pairs press on a bond from both sides it does change geometry: O2 over-binds twofold with a short bond and F2 has no minimum; no choice of rc or shell configuration repairs these (article, Sections on X2, CH4 and Limitations). Energies are quotable within one composition, and for processes joining fragments without changing any atom's bonding — the next step.
