DESCRIPTION  (paste the text below)
------------------------------------------------------
Euler wrote down his equations in 1755 and said that what barred a complete knowledge of the motion of fluids was not mechanics but analysis. The bar held for two and a half centuries: d'Alembert's paradox gave a body moving through an ideal fluid no resistance, while analysis produced smooth solutions describing nothing.

The history is one of what a solution was taken to be. A pointwise solution exists and is unstable, so determines nothing. Leray's weak solution of 1934 exists without uniqueness, so determines nothing either. A weak solution with its residual controlled is not a single flow: the field is undetermined pointwise in space and time, while drag and lift are well determined, each with a computed error bound and confirmed at workshops where predictions precede measurement.

A recent symbolic proof of finite-time blowup, by an AI system, concerns the first concept. Its solution conserves energy exactly and so is not a fluid, and exhibits no individual case to insert, inspect or measure.
