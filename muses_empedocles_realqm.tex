\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The Greeks sought to understand the world through a few fundamental constituents interacting through
a few universal principles. Empedocles named four elements and two forces, Love and Strife. This essay
follows that programme through the history of physics to Real Quantum Mechanics (RealQM), in which
electrons are charge densities in three-dimensional space interacting through Coulomb's law, and asks
whether AI, by taking over the solution of equations, may return physics to its first question: what is
a physically meaningful model of reality?
\end{abstract}

\section{The Muses and the elements}

The ancient Greeks approached both the human mind and the physical world through a small number of
fundamental concepts. The nine Muses represented the forms of intellectual and artistic activity:
Calliope of epic poetry, Clio of history, Erato of love poetry, Euterpe of music, Melpomene of
tragedy, Polyhymnia of sacred song, Terpsichore of dance, Thalia of comedy---and Urania of astronomy~\cite{hesiod}.
That astronomy had its own Muse says something important: science was part of human culture, not a
separate technical enterprise, and understanding nature was bound up with the perception of beauty,
harmony and order.

\section{Empedocles: four roots and two forces}

Around 450\,BC Empedocles of Akragas in Sicily proposed that everything is composed of four
\emph{roots}---the word \emph{element} came later---namely earth, water, air and fire. Their mixing
and separation are governed by two opposing principles:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Love} (\emph{Philotes}), which brings things together;
\item \textbf{Strife} (\emph{Neikos}), which drives them apart~\cite{dk,krs,inwood}.
\end{itemize}
This is a remarkably modern form of physical theory: a few fundamental constituents, interacting through
universal principles, generate the immense diversity of the observable world. Empedocles wrote his theory
as a poem and invoked the Muse Calliope to assist him~\cite{dk}. Physics, philosophy and poetry were still one
enterprise.

\section{Love and Strife as gravitation and Coulomb interaction}

In the physical world envisaged by RealQM two interactions take centre stage:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gravitation}: attraction between masses;
\item \textbf{Coulomb interaction}: attraction between opposite charges, repulsion between equal ones.
\end{itemize}
Gravitation is Love in its purest classical form: it gathers matter into planets, stars and galaxies.
Coulomb interaction carries both principles. The attraction between positive nuclei and negative
electrons brings matter together; the repulsion between charges of equal sign counteracts unlimited
concentration and organises matter in space. Together with wave dynamics, this interplay of attraction
and repulsion produces the structures of atoms, molecules and condensed matter.

Both laws are inverse-square laws~\cite{newton,coulomb}, and both can be written as Poisson equations in three-dimensional
space,
\begin{equation*}
\Delta\phi = 4\pi G\rho_{\mathrm{mass}}, \qquad -\Delta V = 4\pi\rho_{\mathrm{charge}},
\end{equation*}
so that across 2{,}500 years there is a conceptual line from Love and Strife to gravitation and Coulomb
interaction. It is not a literal identification, but a shared vision of nature organised by a few
universal principles of interaction.

\section{Strife has two faces}

Recent computation adds a twist that Empedocles might have enjoyed. Coulomb repulsion is not the whole of
Strife.

Take two helium atoms. Each is a closed shell: two electrons of opposite spin forming a tight pair. In
nature two helium atoms brought close repel each other; there is no He$_2$ molecule in the ordinary
sense. In the basic form of RealQM, where electron domains meet at free walls, the computation instead
finds a false bond of about half a hartree at a separation of two bohr~\cite{rqmol}. Coulomb repulsion between the
electron clouds is present, but it is not enough.

What is missing is the other face of Strife: \emph{exclusion}~\cite{pauli}. Two electrons of the same spin cannot
occupy the same region of space. In RealQM this is not an abstract symmetry of a wave function but a
spatial fact: electrons of the same spin meet at a wall, and the wall must carry a cost---the energy of
the part of the electron's density that the wall cuts off. With that cost, set by the electron's own
energy and nothing fitted, the false bond disappears and helium repels as it should. The same holds for
two beryllium atoms.

So in RealQM Love is Coulomb attraction, and Strife comes in two forms: Coulomb repulsion between
charges, and exclusion between electrons of the same spin. Both are statements about matter in
three-dimensional space.

\section{From Empedocles to modern physics}

The history of physics can be read as a sequence of attempts to carry out the programme of Empedocles:
\begin{itemize}
\item Democritus: atoms and the void;
\item Newton: matter interacting through gravitation;
\item Maxwell: electric and magnetic fields~\cite{maxwell};
\item Einstein: space, time, matter and energy;
\item Schr\"odinger: matter described by wave mechanics~\cite{schrodinger};
\item the Standard Model: elementary particles and quantum fields.
\end{itemize}
Modern physics recognises four fundamental interactions: gravitation, electromagnetism, and the strong
and weak nuclear interactions. RealQM, with its proposed extensions to nuclear physics and cosmology,
explores a more economical programme built on gravitation and Coulomb interaction~\cite{realqm}. Whether such a
reduction can account for the full range of observed phenomena is an open scientific question.

\section{Standard quantum mechanics: simplicity lost?}

Standard quantum mechanics (stdQM), originating with Schr\"odinger in 1926, gives a formally compact
description of atoms and molecules. But the wave function of $N$ electrons depends on $3N$ spatial
coordinates, not on the three coordinates of physical space. Its direct numerical representation
requires resources growing exponentially with the number of electrons---Kohn's ``exponential wall''~\cite{kohn99}--- and a century after its
introduction the physical meaning of a function on $3N$-dimensional configuration space remains disputed.

This is a striking contrast to the programme of Empedocles: a formally simple equation produces a
representation of nature of enormous computational complexity.

Density functional theory (DFT)~\cite{hk,ks} brings the computation back to three dimensions, and it is today the
workhorse of chemistry and materials science. But it does so by collecting everything that cannot be
computed into one unknown term, the exchange--correlation functional, which is defined through the
$3N$-dimensional wave function and is in practice a chosen formula. DFT's own leading developers note
that there is no systematic way to improve these formulas, and that some fitted to energies have drifted
away from the physics~\cite{burke,medvedev}. DFT gives good numbers; it does not claim that its objects are physical. It is a
three-dimensional computational method for a theory posed in $3N$ dimensions.

\section{RealQM: recovering the simplicity of nature}

Real Quantum Mechanics (RealQM)~\cite{realqm} is an attempt to recover the original ambition of natural philosophy
with the means of modern computational mathematics. In RealQM:
\begin{itemize}
\item electrons are charge densities distributed in three-dimensional physical space;
\item electrons occupy non-overlapping domains with free, moving boundaries;
\item electrons and nuclei interact through Coulomb attraction and repulsion, each electron with the
others but not with itself;
\item atomic and molecular dynamics are governed by time-dependent partial differential equations in
physical space.
\end{itemize}
Instead of one wave function in $3N$ dimensions, an $N$-electron system is represented by $N$ interacting
three-dimensional densities. The computational work can then grow essentially linearly with the number
of electrons, given suitable algorithms.

Every object in RealQM is offered as physical, and every approximation is a statement about how
electrons behave. That makes the model testable piece by piece. When the basic model bound two helium
atoms, the failure pointed to one specific claim---that a wall between electrons costs nothing---and the
correction was a physical rule. This is the difference between a model of physics, whose wrong numbers
point to the physics that is missing, and a model for numbers, which can be refitted indefinitely without
teaching anything about nature. The first is science in the sense of Empedocles; the second, however
useful, is closer to Ptolemy's epicycles.

RealQM's present numbers are honest rather than final. For simple molecules its bond lengths are within
a few per cent of experiment, at the level of the simplest density functionals, while its binding
energies are still off by tens of per cent~\cite{rqmol}. The validity of the programme must be settled by
computation and comparison with observation.

In the broader picture Coulomb interaction organises matter on the small scale and gravitation on the
large. From molecules to galaxies, nature would be understood as matter in physical space, interacting
through two laws.

\section{AI and the revival of natural philosophy}

AI adds a new dimension to this story. Mathematical modelling of the world has three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item formulating a question about the world;
\item expressing it in a mathematical model;
\item solving the resulting equations.
\end{enumerate}
AI is rapidly transforming the third step and increasingly assists with the second. This may shift the
attention of physics back to the first and most fundamental question: \emph{what is a physically
meaningful mathematical model of reality?}

The challenge is then not only to solve the equations of stdQM more efficiently, but to ask whether other
equations give a simpler, more transparent and more computable description of the physical world. AI
makes it possible to explore and test such alternatives at a pace that was unthinkable a few years ago:
the work on RealQM reported above---testing a hypothesis, finding where it fails, locating the missing
physics and testing the correction---was carried out in a dialogue between a human and an AI.

\section{Back to the nine Muses}

The nine Muses remind us that understanding involves not only logic and calculation but imagination,
intuition, beauty and creativity. Empedocles reminds us that the ambition of physics is to explain the
complex through the simple. Newton and Coulomb gave attraction and repulsion precise mathematical form;
RealQM seeks to build on these laws through computational models in physical space; and AI may now
provide the means to explore that vision on an unprecedented scale.

Could the era of AI bring a renaissance of natural philosophy, uniting physical intuition, mathematical
modelling and computational exploration? Could RealQM be a step in that direction?

After 2{,}500 years the vision of Empedocles remains remarkably attractive: the immense complexity of the
world emerging from a few constituents interacting through Love and Strife.
