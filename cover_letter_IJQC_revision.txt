To Dr. Felix Plasser, Handling Editor
International Journal of Quantum Chemistry

Dear Dr. Plasser,

I resubmit manuscript 1568909, now titled "A 3D Multiphase Continuum Model
for Atoms and the Periodic Table", in response to the two referee reports.
A point-by-point response accompanies this letter.

The revision is substantial, and in one respect it goes further than a reply
to the reports. Both referees pressed on the same place: the calibrated core
radius r_c and the per-period constants it carried, and the ionization
energies built on them. On checking those objections I found them correct,
and more than correct. The core radius, the pseudo-kernel and the two-scale
core/valence decomposition are gone; every atom is now solved all-electron
with the bare nuclear term, no fitted length anywhere. All
ionization-energy material has been removed, including the Koopmans
identification the second referee questioned in Eq. (1). The "4s before 3d"
claim rested on a shell the generating code placed outermost by
construction, and it is withdrawn. So is the claim that the model
over-binds the heavier third-period atoms with the opposite sign of error to
Hartree-Fock: argon's error changes sign between N=400 and N=1024, so the
original statement was an artefact of one mesh.

What replaces that material is a different kind of claim, and it is why the
title has been extended. The model has exactly two filled packings, the
antipodal pair and the 4+4 octet of two dual tetrahedra, and the row
capacities of the periodic table are sums of those two and nothing else.
A period ends when its capacity is exhausted. That construction is
checkable by composition rather than weighed against a fitted number, and I
would ask that the manuscript be judged on it. The computed scope now runs
from hydrogen to krypton, periods 1 to 4, all-electron, with the nuclear
charge Z as the only quantity supplied.

I have tried to be equally plain about what does not work. The shell filling
is derived from the build-up only to carbon; beyond carbon it is supplied,
and that is stated as the paper's principal limitation. The repetition of
each capacity across two periods is reproduced but not derived, and the
geometric argument I had offered for it is shown in the text to be
equivalent to its own conclusion and is withdrawn as a derivation. A
measurement that bears on it has been made and is reported with its limits
rather than as a result.

The abstract has been rewritten to 149 words, within the 150-word
guideline, with no bold-face elements and no numerical literature
references. A graphical abstract image and a 75-word table-of-contents
summary accompany the submission.

I am grateful to both referees. The paper is better for the objections, and
in the places where they were right the material is gone rather than
defended.

Yours sincerely,

Claes Johnson
