NOTE TO MODERATORS  (only if contacted; not part of the submission)

  An earlier version of this submission was declined. I believe the reason
  was the title, which named the Clay Millennium Problem and so read as a
  claim to have solved it. The paper makes no such claim, and says so
  explicitly in Section 1: the method is directed at alternative (A), and
  what it delivers is constructive existence and qualitative uniqueness of
  turbulent solutions, not the global smoothness that (A) requires. The
  solutions in question are not smooth -- their gradients grow like
  nu^{-1/2} while their velocities stay bounded -- and the paper states
  this as a limitation rather than working around it.

  The title has been changed to state the result instead of naming the
  problem, and the primary class moved from math.AP to math.NA, which
  better fits the content: a finite element method with output error control
  from a dual-weighted residual, implemented in FEniCS, applied to
  turbulent flow at high Reynolds number.

  The underlying material is not new. It was published in 2007 as Chapter 14
  of "Computational Turbulent Incompressible Flow" (Hoffman and Johnson,
  Springer, Applied Mathematics: Body and Soul, vol. 4), with the supporting
  computational record in refereed journals from 2006 onward, all cited.

  I am endorsed for submission.
