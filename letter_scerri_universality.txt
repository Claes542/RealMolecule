Dear Eric,

I sent you the revised atoms manuscript when it was ready. This is a short note to
add the companion piece that came out of that referee round, in case it interests you.
There is nothing I am asking for -- no reply needed.

The referees pressed me on why the model uses fourfold rather than eightfold packing,
and the only answer I had was that it agreed better with reference energies, which is a
reason to prefer a number rather than a reason for the number to be what it is.
Answering it properly meant saying what makes eight a closed shell, and that turned into
a separate paper on a more general question: what grounds the claim that quantum
mechanics is universal for chemistry.

Dirac put the claim in its strongest form in 1929 -- the laws sufficient for "the whole
of chemistry" are completely known, "and the difficulty is only that the exact
application of these laws leads to equations much too complicated to be soluble." Both
halves in one sentence, with the second treated as an inconvenience. My argument is that
the two together make the claim untestable: in 3N dimensions the equation is uncomputable
past a few tens of electrons, so what is ever compared with measurement is a reduction
carrying parameters, and the approximation hierarchy cannot carry verification upward
because its exact limit is the uncomputable object itself. Hence the practice in which a
dispersion failure is charged to the functional and repaired with a fitted term, and
never to the equation.

Two things I should say so you do not have to look for them. The target is the
N-electron equation itself, not the orbital account of the table -- the hydrogenic
s,p,d,f scheme in Madelung order is an independent-particle approximation laid over the
equation, it is the easier target, and I am not aiming at it. And the equation's
correctness is not in question; what I examine is the universality claim made on its
behalf, which is an assertion about scope and about what can be held to account rather
than about truth.

On the periodic table I claim less than you might expect. Each row length decomposes
into pairs and octets, uniquely, with the pair present when n is odd. I do not claim the
row lengths follow from the model -- the generator is given them, and the build-up is
derived only as far as carbon. Period doubling I leave open, as it is in the orbital
account. And 4+4 is Linnett's double quartet, not mine; what differs is the route, since
there is no spin in the model and the decomposition comes from Coulomb interaction and
non-overlap alone.

Your own view -- that quantum mechanics has not in fact explained the periodic table --
is one I lean on rather than dispute, and that is mostly why I thought you would want to
see this.
