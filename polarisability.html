Polarisability: the one number with an experiment on the other side
A single atom in a uniform field. The induced dipole divided by the field is the static polarisability α, and for the noble gases it is known to four figures.
Why this and not a barrier. Everything else measured in this project is a difference between configurations, compared against another calculation. α is a response, compared against spectroscopy. It is also the same quantity that decides conduction: a bound electron has ω02=K/m and a finite ε=1+ωp2/ω02; a free one has ω0=0, ε divergent, and σ≠0. A finite polarisability is the statement that the system does not conduct, quantified.
Reference values (a03): He 1.383, Ne 2.669, Ar 11.08, H 4.50, Li 164.1. The spread across that list is a factor of 100, so agreement to a factor of two would already be meaningful and disagreement by ten would be decisive.
What is measured. The electronic charge centroid from the density itself, not from the partition — with the boundary frozen the partition cannot move at all, so a partition centroid would report zero response no matter what the field did. p = −∫ρx dV relative to the nucleus, and α = p/F fitted over several fields, which also tests linearity: α must be the same at every F or the response is not yet in the linear regime.
